# GRACEFELL v2.24 release record — impact frames

Date: 2026-07-26
Owner: @jonathanwxh-cell
Implementation and verification: Claude (Opus 5 / Claude Code)

Status: local acceptance complete

## Outcome

Three presentation-only changes to how a hit reads:

1. **Sword trail** — one continuously tapered filled strip instead of up to nine
   constant-width stroked segments. The old loop stepped `lineWidth` and alpha
   per segment, so the arc showed banding at its joins.
2. **Player impact frame** — a swing stretches the body along its facing and
   squashes it across, peaking at the start and decaying over 0.16 s.
3. **Boss hit recoil** — the drawn body gives ground along the blow and settles
   back. The shadow stays planted, so the boss reads as absorbing the hit.

Combat timing, damage, poise, stamina, invulnerability, boss AI, difficulty,
input, scoring, persistence, save schema v7, weather, music, and sound effects
are unchanged. Nothing here feeds back into simulation.

## What was dropped, and why

A boss silhouette rim-light was scoped and then cut. `design-qa.md` establishes
the boss's read as "broken blade halo first, split ash cape second"; a rim would
compete with the halo for the same edge. The player already carries a rim at
close range, which is what actually solves player/boss separation at the 0.55
mobile camera, so the boss rim was redundant as well as risky.

## Why this did not need a performance budget

v2.23 measured a worst-case phase-three frame at ~1.7 ms of a 16.7 ms budget and
established that the draw path is not the constraint. This release was chosen on
merit rather than on cost. It happens to be cheaper anyway: the trail is one
`fill` where it was up to nine `stroke` calls, and the impact frame is transform
maths with no extra draw.

`qa/perf.cjs` gates the result — gradients per projectile, `shadowBlur` count,
and draw calls are all unchanged from the v2.23 baseline.

## The silhouette risk, and how it was handled

`design-qa.md` commits the player to seven authored macro-silhouettes that must
survive a 0.55 camera. An anisotropic scale could have broken that contract.

The impact frame is therefore applied to the **transform around**
`drawKiteVeilBody`, never to the authored path data — the state shapes
themselves are untouched. Captured mid-swing at 3.2× zoom, the parchment kite
and veil mass stay legible at peak stretch; the pose reads as a lunge.

## Acceptance

- `src/game/engine.test.ts` — `attackStretchImpulse` is a pure exported function
  with 5 new tests covering the non-attacking states, the decay curve, the 0..1
  bound, and all three attack verbs. 24 tests total.
- `qa/v224.cjs` — a new lane proving the presentation-only claim:
  - a hit records recoil **without** moving `boss.x/y`;
  - the boss does not move while the recoil decays;
  - recoil returns to rest, and a fresh fight does not inherit it;
  - three `render()` calls across a live swing mutate **no** simulation state
    (position, timing, hp, stamina, poise, particle and damage-number counts).
- **The lane was mutation-tested.** Injecting `this.x += Math.cos(a) * 2` into
  `Boss.takeDamage` made it fail with
  `boss position moved on hit (render-only violated)`. An assertion that cannot
  fail is not worth committing.
- `npm run lint` clean · `npm test` 24/24 · `npm run build` clean
- `npm run qa` — verify, v221, v224, and perf lanes all pass

## Known limits

- Screenshots were taken at 3.2× zoom to make the trail legible in a still. The
  effect has **not** been reviewed at the real 0.55 mobile camera by a human, and
  it has not been playtested on a phone. Whether the impact frame reads as feel
  or as noise at true play scale is an owner judgement.
- The stretch constants (0.17 along facing, 0.11 across, 0.16 s) are authored,
  not tuned against play. They are the obvious thing to adjust first.
